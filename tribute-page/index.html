Sheikh Mujibur Rahman

The founding father of Bangladesh.

Timeline of Sheikh Mujibur Rahman's Life:
Year Event
1920 Born in Tungipara, a small village in the Gopalganj District of Bengal (then British India).
1940 Joins the All India Muslim Students Federation while studying in Calcutta, beginning his lifelong political journey.
1947 Opposes the partition plan that neglects Bengali interests. Supports the Pakistan movement but insists on protecting Bengali rights in the new nation.
1949 Becomes one of the founding members of the Awami Muslim League, which later becomes the Awami League – the key political party in Bangladesh’s struggle for independence.
1952 Plays a key role behind the scenes in the Language Movement, demanding Bengali be recognized as a state language of Pakistan. Imprisoned for his activism.
1954 Wins a seat in the East Bengal Legislative Assembly during provincial elections. His popularity begins to rise rapidly in East Pakistan.
1966 Presents the Six-Point Movement, a bold proposal for regional autonomy that becomes the blueprint for Bangladesh’s future independence. The Pakistani regime responds with repression.
1969 After mass protests, is released from jail and is hailed as “Bangabandhu” (Friend of Bengal) by the people. The title sticks.
1970 Leads the Awami League to a landslide victory in Pakistan’s general elections, winning 160 out of 162 East Pakistan seats — a clear majority in the national assembly. The West Pakistani leadership refuses to transfer power.
1971 Delivers his historic March 7 speech in Dhaka: “The struggle this time is the struggle for our emancipation! The struggle this time is the struggle for our independence!” Later that month, he is arrested and flown to West Pakistan as the Liberation War begins.
1972 Released from jail after the war is won and Bangladesh is born. Returns to a hero’s welcome in Dhaka and becomes the first Prime Minister of Bangladesh.
1974 Signs Bangladesh’s first constitution and works to rebuild the war-torn nation amid famine and economic crisis.
1975 Declares a shift to one-party rule under BAKSAL, aiming for unity but facing criticism. On August 15, 1975, he is assassinated in a military coup, along with most of his family.

“A great light has gone out of our lives and there is darkness everywhere.”

— Indira Gandhi on the assassination of Sheikh Mujibur Rahman, August 15, 1975
